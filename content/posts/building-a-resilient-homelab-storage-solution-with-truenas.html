The short version

TrueNAS SCALE runs as a VM on Proxmox at two sites. The main site has a 4-drive RAIDZ1 pool plus a single 8TB drive for local backups, and nightly rsync over WireGuard feeds the second site. It's a production system holding my family's data, so it gets scrubs, snapshots and a backup I can actually restore from.

This is not a drill

This is not a weekend proof-of-concept. My friends and family rely on the services I host (for free, of course), and those services store critical data on my TrueNAS servers. You don't want to explain to your partner why the family photos are gone, or why their Time Machine backup vanished. Phones record 4K video where a 30-second clip can run to hundreds of megabytes, and cloud storage gets expensive fast as your data grows.

3-2-1, and redundancy is not a backup

The 3-2-1 rule: 3 copies of your data, on 2 different types of media, with 1 copy off-site.

Redundancy is not a backupRAID is great for availability. It won't save you from file corruption, accidental deletion or ransomware. A backup is a separate, versioned copy you can restore from.

The layout

Both sites run Proxmox with TrueNAS SCALE in a VM. At the primary site, "Darkshield", I pass an entire Intel SATA controller through to the TrueNAS VM (PCIe passthrough). That gives TrueNAS raw access to the drives, which ZFS needs. The secondary site, "StarkAI", also runs a TrueNAS VM and mostly exists to receive backups. The sites are joined by an encrypted WireGuard tunnel, and nightly rsync jobs copy the critical datasets across.

Why the local backup matters

An off-site backup is the safety net for fire or theft, but it's a poor way to recover quickly. The bottleneck is almost always your internet connection. Try your own numbers:

Data to restore TB at Mbps

My worst case: lose 20TB on the primary, with a residential line averaging 300 Mbps, roughly 37.5 MB/s. That is 20,480,000 MB divided by 37.5 MB/s, about 546,133 seconds, or 6.3 days. Sustained speeds vary with congestion and ISP throttling, so that is the realistic version, not the gigabit marketing one. Over my local gigabit network, restoring terabytes takes hours, not weeks.

ZFS for humans

  • ZFS is a file system and logical volume manager in one. It covers data integrity, snapshots and RAID-like functionality.
  • vdevs are the building blocks of a pool: a single drive, a mirror, or a RAIDZ array.
  • Cache (L2ARC) is an optional fast SSD for frequently read data.
  • Logs (ZIL) is a dedicated fast drive, like an NVMe SSD, that speeds up synchronous writes.

It's copy-on-write, which makes it resistant to corruption, and it has built-in snapshots and replication.

Keeping it healthy

Scrub tasks run on a schedule on both TrueNAS instances to check data integrity, and I take frequent snapshots, which are read-only copies of a dataset I can roll back to instantly. If this goes down I risk losing critical data. That is a sobering thought and a good motivator.

Why not just buy a NAS

Control and hardware utilization. A custom server lets me run Proxmox and put storage next to other VMs and containers. A Synology or QNAP is often a closed box with limited, often inferior, hardware and software. It's also a good way to learn, and you end up with something that's yours.
