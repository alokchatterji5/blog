The short version

Four layers: a single entry point, a shared identity layer, the services themselves, and observability that watches everything and changes nothing. No tools named, because the shape is what you can reuse.

No configs in this one, just the shape of the thing. If you're building your own version, the shape is what transfers. The specific tools you pick won't be mine, and they shouldn't be.

One front door

Every request into the homelab goes through a single point first. Not because it's the only valid way, but because one front door means exactly one place that governs how traffic reaches anything: one place to reason about, to secure, to change. Services don't get their own way in. If it's not through the front door, it doesn't happen.

This came out of the architecture conversation in part one. "No service should be its own special case" was a principle we agreed on early, and the network layer is where it gets enforced.

One decision about who gets in

Just behind the front door is identity. Who you are and what you're allowed to touch is decided once, in one place, and every service downstream inherits that decision instead of implementing its own login and rules. It turns "forty services with forty different logins" into "one identity, consistently enforced."

This is also where I was most deliberate about not automating my own judgment away. AI can draft how a new service plugs into this layer. It doesn't get to decide the policy.

The actual homelab

Everything above is scaffolding. The service layer is the point of the project: media, tools, personal apps, whatever the lab exists to do. It stays simple because it doesn't have to solve networking or identity. It shows up, plugs into the front door and identity layer that already exist, and inherits both.

It's also the layer that grows fastest and where AI does the most hands-on work, because almost everything here is additive and reversible. New service, new container. Nothing threatens what's already running.

A layer that watches without touching

Wrapped around all of it is observability: it sees everything and can change nothing. That asymmetry is deliberate. When something is off, this is how I find out before a person does, not after.

Why this shape, and not a shopping list

The point isn't "use these four categories of software." A homelab built without this separation grows sideways: every new service solves its own auth, routing and visibility until nothing is consistent and everything is fragile. With the separation, it stays legible as it grows, because new things slot into an existing shape instead of inventing their own.

That shape is also what makes AI-assisted building work well. Clear separation of concerns keeps a wrong call in one place from rippling everywhere else.
