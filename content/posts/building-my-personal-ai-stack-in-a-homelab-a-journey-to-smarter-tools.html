The short version

LiteLLM sits in the middle and routes every request, either to local models in Ollama or to cloud providers. Obsidian, VS Code, Nextcloud, Home Assistant and n8n all talk to it. A GTX 1660 Super does the local work.

Most AI tools are cloud-locked and capped by usage. I wanted something private and local that I could shape, for brainstorming, coding, automation and organising my life. This is how it is put together. Click a box to see what it does.

The core four

  • Ollama runs LLMs locally on my hardware. It is efficient and supports open models like LLaMA and Mistral.
  • OpenWeb-UI is the chat front end. It connects to Ollama or goes through LiteLLM. I like the clean design, the chat history and the plugins.
  • LiteLLM is the API orchestrator: key and quota management, routing between local and cloud models, and load balancing across models and endpoints. It handles multiple OpenAI and Gemini keys.
  • Stable Diffusion generates images from local models, wired into OpenWeb-UI for text-to-image, with nothing sent to the cloud.

What I use it for

  • Obsidian: AI plugins for content, summaries and brainstorming in my notes.
  • VS Code: the Cline extension for code generation, debugging help and explanations. ChatGPT-style, but self-hosted.
  • Nextcloud: summarising documents, writing reports and generating slides, privately.
  • Home Assistant: talking to the house in natural language, like "Turn off all the lights and summarize today's news."
  • n8n: automated workflows such as auto-generated replies, email summaries and blog outlines from notes.
  • Experiments: a test bench for PDF summarisers, chatbots and creative writing tools, quickly and without limits.
