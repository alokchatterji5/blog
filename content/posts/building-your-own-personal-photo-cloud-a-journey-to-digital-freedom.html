The short version

My family's photos live on a TrueNAS server at home, get copied locally, and replicate every night to a second TrueNAS in another location over WireGuard. Immich is the main phone app, Nextcloud Photos runs alongside it, and I pay no recurring fee.

Google Photos and iCloud both want a bigger plan sooner or later, or a lower photo quality. I wanted storage with no ceiling, my own rules, and access for family and close friends without handing each of them a subscription. It also turned out to be a good way to learn server management and network security, which I did not mind.

The setup

Two TrueNAS servers in different places. The primary holds the important data in a RAID10 dataset. A second dataset on the same box keeps a local copy, so a recovery does not mean pulling everything over the internet. Every night a batch job replicates the important data to the second server, over SSH keys inside a WireGuard tunnel.

Two apps, on purpose

For phone backup I run Immich and Nextcloud Photos (the Memories app) side by side. Photos are the one thing I cannot recreate, so I would rather have two working paths than trust one. It also lets me compare them. So far Immich is winning.

Open-source, self-hosted, and a modern stand-in for Google Photos. Mobile backup can be set to Wi-Fi only and charging only, and the interface is a pleasure to use.

The standout is the AI side. It uses my local AI stack and the server GPU for face and object recognition. I find its face recognition better and more accurate than Google Photos, and it groups faces and finds objects without fuss.

Photos are one piece of Nextcloud, which also does file sync, calendar, contacts and document editing, like a self-hosted Dropbox or Google Drive. It has the same Wi-Fi and charging options for mobile backup, a big app ecosystem, and is wired to my AI stack and GPU for face and object recognition too.

Both sign in with Google OAuth, so nobody has to remember another password.

What is not great

Honest costs You need real technical knowledge to set this up and keep it running. Self-hosting carries a risk of data loss if you manage it badly; the backup layout above is how I cut that down. And a homelab will not match the uptime of a commercial provider.

Next on my list is load balancing the services across several servers, mainly to improve uptime and responsiveness for Immich and Nextcloud.

The effort up front is real. The payoff is storage with no ceiling, full control, and no monthly bill for my own photos.
